# Amazon KDP publishing requirements for AI-generated children's booklets

**Research date:** 2026-08-31  
**Source access date:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Product-planning research  
**Scope:** Amazon Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP) policy and publishing guidance

> This document is research for product planning. It is not legal advice. KDP
> rules are provider policies, not statements of applicable law. A qualified
> lawyer must review questions about copyright ownership, publishing rights,
> privacy, publicity rights, and other legal obligations before production use
> or publication.

## Purpose and limits

This note records recent KDP research that is relevant if a booklet created by
the Kids Booklet POC is later published through KDP. Publication is outside the
current POC scope. The POC exports a PDF but does not publish, distribute, or sell
the booklet.

The conclusions below describe Amazon's current platform requirements and
guidance. They do not resolve the wider legal questions in issue #23. In
particular, they do not establish that a user owns copyright in AI-generated
content or has permission to use a name, likeness, trademark, or reference work.

## Verified KDP policy requirements

### AI-content disclosure

**Classification:** Provider-policy requirement.

KDP requires a publisher to disclose AI-generated text, images, and translations
when publishing a new book or republishing an edited book. The requirement covers
cover and interior artwork. KDP treats content as AI-generated when an AI tool
created the content, even if a person made substantial edits later.

KDP does not require disclosure when AI only helped a person edit, refine, check,
or brainstorm content that the person ultimately created. The publisher remains
responsible for compliance with KDP content rules and applicable intellectual
property rights in both cases.

**POC implication:** Booklet text and images generated by the POC are
AI-generated under KDP's definition. A future KDP publishing flow must tell the
publisher to disclose both types. The POC must not describe substantially edited
AI output as only AI-assisted.

### Publishing rights and intellectual property

**Classification:** Provider-policy requirement with related legal risk.

KDP requires the publisher to hold the publishing rights for all uploaded
content. KDP can reject or remove a book, affect the account, or withhold
royalties when the publisher does not hold those rights. KDP can request evidence
of rights. A copyright page in the book is not sufficient evidence.

KDP also makes the publisher responsible for avoiding violations of copyright,
trademark, brand, privacy, publicity, and other rights. KDP's guidance does not
determine whether a particular AI-generated work is protected by copyright or who
owns it.

**POC implication:** A future publication workflow needs a rights confirmation
that covers prompts, generated text, generated images, fonts, uploaded assets,
names, likenesses, and any third-party references. It should retain the evidence
that supports each confirmation. A copyright notice must not be presented as
proof of rights.

### Content and customer experience

**Classification:** Provider-policy requirement.

KDP can reject or remove content that violates its content guidelines or creates
a poor customer experience. The rules apply to the book, title, cover, metadata,
and product description. KDP prohibits content that is illegal or infringing and
certain offensive content, including content that promotes child abuse or sexual
exploitation.

KDP also identifies quality failures that can cause warnings, rejection, or
removal. Relevant failures include unreadable or blurry images, illegible text in
images, missing or duplicated content, a mismatch between the uploaded file and
its metadata, and misleading descriptions.

Children's picture books are excluded from KDP's general objection to books made
entirely from scanned or image-captured text. Their images and lettering must
still be clear and readable.

**POC implication:** Publication readiness requires a separate quality gate after
PDF generation. The gate should compare the title and metadata with the actual
book, check for missing or duplicate pages, and inspect every page at the intended
display and print sizes. Passing the POC's PDF export checks does not by itself
show compliance with KDP policy.

### Image preparation for print

**Classification:** Provider technical guidance.

KDP recommends that print images are inserted at their original size, flattened
to one layer, and prepared at a minimum of 300 DPI. KDP classifies images below
200 DPI as low resolution. Changing only the DPI metadata does not add pixels or
improve image quality. KDP also advises publishers to use proof copies to inspect
paperback or hardcover output.

**POC implication:** The exporter should keep enough source pixels for 300 DPI at
the selected physical trim size. A future print workflow should flatten
transparency, avoid enlarging raster images after generation, and verify a proof
copy. DPI is an output-size calculation, not only a file metadata value.

### Kindle format and preview checks

**Classification:** Provider technical guidance.

KDP publishes separate guidance for reflowable and fixed-layout Kindle books.
Children's illustrated books can require a fixed-layout workflow. KDP recommends
reviewing eBooks with Kindle Previewer and testing navigation, formatting,
lettering, and images on the intended reading experience.

KDP's children's-book page describes supported preparation and publishing paths,
including its illustrated-book tooling. Tool availability and supported formats
can change. A publisher must confirm the current workflow when preparing a real
submission.

**POC implication:** The current PDF is not automatically a valid or
quality-assured Kindle eBook. Kindle export should remain a separate future
feature with its own format conversion, preview, and device checks.

### Primary audience and reading age

**Classification:** Provider metadata requirement and guidance.

KDP requires an answer to its sexually explicit image or title question during
title setup. A `Yes` answer makes the book ineligible for Children's categories.
The reading-age field is optional, but omitting an appropriate range can make a
children's book ineligible for children's categories and age-specific search.

KDP identifies these audience bands:

| Reading age | KDP example |
| --- | --- |
| 0-2 | Simple picture books with few or no words |
| 3-5 | Picture books |
| 6-8 | Early readers and first chapter books |
| 9-12 | Middle-grade chapter books |
| 13-17 | Teen and young-adult chapter books |

Amazon can later display a customer-recommended reading age instead of the
publisher's selection.

**POC implication:** The booklet's approved Audience Profile should map to the KDP
reading-age bands before publication. That mapping must not silently change the
creative age band or claim that Amazon will retain the publisher's value.

## Recommended POC-safe constraints

These controls are recommendations, not current POC acceptance criteria:

1. Preserve whether each exported text and image asset is AI-generated.
2. Show an explicit publication warning that KDP requires disclosure of generated
   text and images.
3. Do not claim that generation or editing gives the user copyright or publishing
   rights.
4. Preserve source dimensions and calculate effective DPI for the selected print
   size.
5. Require a separate human review for rights, age suitability, metadata accuracy,
   image clarity, lettering readability, and page completeness before publication.
6. Treat Kindle conversion, KDP metadata entry, proof review, and submission as
   future publishing work outside this POC.

## Open questions for qualified review

- What copyright, contractual, or other rights can a publisher claim in each
  generated text and image under the applicable jurisdiction and provider terms?
- What evidence is sufficient to support rights in prompts, generated assets,
  fonts, and any third-party material?
- What consents are required if a booklet uses a real person's name or likeness,
  especially a child's likeness?
- Which disclosures should the product show to users in addition to KDP's private
  submission disclosure?
- Which records should be retained to support a rights challenge, and for how long?
- Do the chosen text and image provider terms permit the intended commercial
  publication in all selected territories?
