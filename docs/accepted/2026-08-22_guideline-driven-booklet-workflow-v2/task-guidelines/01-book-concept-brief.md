# Guideline — Generating a Book Concept Brief

## Purpose

Turn the user's first free-form input into the concise, high-impact creative foundation for the whole booklet. This artifact captures what must shape every later decision without writing the full story, detailed cast, visual design, or page plan.

## Required inputs

- The user's initial prompt in any form.
- Target reader age or age range.
- Any stated audience, theme, premise, representation, family, cultural, safety, tone, or learning requirements.

Ask only for a missing decision that would materially change the book; target age is always required. Preserve user-provided constraints faithfully and distinguish hard requirements from open creative territory.

## Required artifact

```markdown
# Book Concept Brief — [Working Title]

**Status:** Draft awaiting approval

## Source Input
## Intended Audience
## Core Premise
## Theme and Emotional Promise
## Story Scope and Tone
## Representation, Safety, and Content Requirements
## Known Constraints
## Intentionally Deferred Decisions
## Approval Questions
```

## Method

1. Restate the user's idea faithfully in one sentence.
2. If the overall direction is open, offer two or three concise, meaningfully different concept options rather than inventing details silently.
3. Create a brief that states the audience, central story promise, theme, emotional goal, tone, constraints, and what is intentionally undecided.
4. Do not introduce a detailed plot, character appearance, named cast, exact locations, page count, visual style, or layout.
5. Present the brief as a draft and obtain explicit approval.

## Quality gate

- The target audience is explicit.
- A later agent can understand what the book is about, why it matters, and what constraints it must preserve.
- The artifact remains concise and does not pre-empt later work.
