# Guideline — Generating a Page-by-Page Manuscript

**Artifact:** Reusable generation guideline for Artifact 03
**Status:** Draft awaiting approval

## Purpose

Create the complete, child-facing page-by-page story from an approved Book Concept Brief and Story Outline. The manuscript is the narrative source for later cast, location, pagination, and illustration decisions; it is not a visual-design or layout document.

## Required Inputs

- Approved Book Concept Brief
- Approved Story Outline
- Target reader age and reading context
- Any already-approved project decisions that must be preserved as exceptions (for example, a pre-existing character package)

Do not assume unapproved visual details, named characters, locations, page count, or a graphic style.

## Required Output Template

Start every document with:

```markdown
# Page-by-Page Manuscript — *[Book Title]*

**Artifact:** 03 — Page-by-Page Manuscript
**Status:** Draft awaiting approval
**Audience:** [age range]
**Source:** [approved inputs]
```

Then include:

1. **Purpose and Scope** — State that the sequence is not yet a physical pagination or illustration plan.
2. **Story Text and Production Requirements** — For each provisional page or spread, include:
   - page/spread number and brief beat title;
   - polished child-facing narration and only necessary dialogue;
   - required story action;
   - characters required;
   - setting required.
3. **Manuscript Continuity Checks** — Verify cause and effect, character representation, safety, emotional arc, and deferred decisions.
4. **Derived Design Inputs After Approval** — Identify only the next artifacts that the manuscript will drive: Character List and Required Locations List.
5. **Approval Gate** — Request approval or page-specific revisions before downstream design work begins.

## Generation Method

1. Extract the irreversible story commitments from the concept brief and outline.
2. Choose the smallest page-by-page sequence that makes every causal step readable for the target age. Treat this as manuscript sequence only; the Pagination & Layout Plan later decides physical pages, spreads, cover, and text placement.
3. Write read-aloud-ready prose with varied sentence length, concrete actions, emotional clarity, and enough white space to support illustration.
4. Give every page one dominant narrative job. Do not cram multiple turning points into a single page merely to shorten the document.
5. For each page, record only the characters and setting required by the text. Do not write wardrobe, pose, lighting, weather, composition, or image-prompt instructions.
6. Run the checks below before presenting the draft.

## Quality Checks

- The manuscript preserves the approved premise, conflict, resolution, and emotional change.
- Every consequence has a visible cause in an earlier page.
- The protagonist acts through judgement and choice, not an unearned power solution.
- Language fits the stated reader age and sounds natural aloud.
- Supporting characters have story functions beyond pitying, mocking, or admiring the protagonist.
- Any representation requirement is maintained naturally in the story and can be handed to later illustration work without ambiguity.
- The crisis stays within the agreed safety boundary.
- Each location and character entered in the page requirements is actually needed; no speculative world-building is added.
- No visual-style language, time-of-day instruction, weather, camera direction, text-safe area, or design specification leaks into the manuscript unless the story itself genuinely requires it.

## Common Failures to Prevent

| Failure | Prevention |
| --- | --- |
| Treating the manuscript as a layout plan | Keep physical pagination, spreads, cover, typography, and text placement for Artifact 09. |
| Designing characters too soon | Name only required roles unless an approved source already locks a name or identity. Put physical design in individual character packages later. |
| Building a broad setting bible | Record only the setting each approved page requires; derive the Required Locations List after approval. |
| Repeating the outline instead of writing a story | Supply polished child-facing prose for every page, not just beat summaries. |
| Making the hero solve everything effortlessly | Show limits, hesitation, small choices, and adult support where appropriate. |
| Treating disability as a plot repair | Keep it an ordinary part of the character; never turn it into a cure, magic replacement, or moral shortcut. |
| Producing too much prose for illustration-led pages | Keep each page focused, vivid, and read-aloud friendly. |

## Approval and Handoff

The artifact remains **Draft awaiting approval** until the user explicitly approves the complete text. On approval:

1. Promote the manuscript to its canonical approved filename.
2. Create or revise the Character List from the approved page requirements.
3. Create the Required Locations List from the approved page requirements.
4. Do not create new individual character packages, location-definition packages, or page illustration packages until their own dependencies and approval gates are satisfied.
