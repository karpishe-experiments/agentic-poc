# Page 3 of 6 — Story Page 2: The Tiny Envelope

## Page purpose

Shift from the happy routine into a gentle mystery. The image and text must make the tiny blank envelope feel important without making the moment anxious.

## Story moment

Milo’s route is finished. At the bottom of his almost-empty bag, he finds one envelope he cannot deliver because nobody’s name or home is written on it.

## Characters present

- **Milo:** The only full character in the scene. He is quietly puzzled: ears forward, one eyebrow raised, mouth a small “o,” tail in a loose curve.
- **Optional background birds:** One or two small, non-recurring sparrows on a distant roofline only. No other named villagers appear.

## Illustration brief

### Composition

Make this a close, intimate portrait-page scene. Milo sits on a low warm-grey stone wall in the lower centre-left, with his red bicycle parked behind him at a diagonal. His open red satchel is on his lap. He holds the tiny cream envelope between both front paws at chest height, so it is large enough for a child to inspect.

### Foreground action

The envelope is the focal object, second only to Milo’s face. Its front is completely blank: no name, no house drawing, no stamp. A round red wax seal sits near the flap. Milo looks from the blank envelope toward the quiet lane, thinking carefully. His cap remains on his head; his cream shirt and diagonal satchel strap must be visible. The wicker bicycle basket contains a few ordinary envelopes, but no clutter.

### Midground and background

The empty lane curves away between two quiet cottages. A closed bakery awning, garden gate, and distant post office establish that the workday has paused. Use only three background details: a cottage door, a low stone wall with daisies, and the distant post-office sign. The village should feel peaceful rather than deserted.

### Light, palette, and mood

Warm midday light, slightly quieter than Page 2. Use cream paper, red seal, ginger fur, and soft blue shadow to make the envelope clear. The mood is curious and tender, like finding a small secret.

### Complexity limit

**Low.** Milo, his bicycle, his satchel, and the envelope are enough. Do not add other villagers, busy shop activity, multiple letters in the air, or extra comic business.

## Exact page text

At the very bottom of his bag, one tiny envelope waited.

No name.

No house.

Only a red seal, tight and round.

“Someone is waiting for this,” said Milo.

## Text treatment and placement

- The first four lines are narration in a single calm text block in the upper-right sky/wall area.
- Put Milo’s final line in one small speech bubble near his face, with the tail pointing clearly to him.
- Do not place any text on the envelope; its blankness is part of the story.

## Continuity check

- This is the first appearance of the mystery envelope.
- It is cream, landscape-oriented, tiny compared with Milo’s paw, and blank on the front.
- The red seal is present, but the paw-print clue is not yet visible.
