# *Milo the Village Postcat*

## One-line plot

When Milo, the kind village postcat, finds a tiny letter with no address, he follows one small paw-print clue and learns that the whole village has made the letter just for him.

## Book-wide visual and storytelling notes

- **Audience and voice:** Ages 3–5. Read-aloud text is short, warm, and rhythmic. The story can be understood from the pictures even before a child can read the words.
- **Format:** Six full-illustration pages total: one cover and five story pages. Keep all text in open sky, pale walls, paths, or other calm areas; never cover faces or the important action.
- **Milo:** A small, round ginger tabby cat with three darker forehead stripes, bright green eyes, and a curled tail. He wears a navy-blue postman cap with a gold paw badge, a cream shirt, and a red canvas mail satchel. He rides a tiny red bicycle with a wicker front basket.
- **Setting:** Poppywick Village, a cozy countryside village of rounded cottages, flower boxes, cobbled lanes, apple trees, and gentle rolling hills. The palette is sunny and storybook-soft: warm cream, poppy red, leaf green, and sky blue.
- **Visual motif:** A tiny cream envelope with a red wax seal and one little paw print. Place it visibly in Pages 2–5 so children can follow its journey.

---

## Page 1 — Cover

### Story concept

Introduce Milo as the village’s cheerful postcat and establish Poppywick as a place full of friendly animal neighbours. This page promises a gentle, busy delivery-day adventure; it does not need to advance the plot.

### Characters in the illustration

- **Milo:** Riding toward the viewer, one paw on the handlebars and one paw waving.
- **Bunny:** A small white rabbit in a yellow apron, standing outside the bakery with a loaf of bread.
- **Bear:** A kindly brown bear in a green gardening hat, watering oversized daisies.
- **Mouse:** A tiny grey mouse peeking from a cottage window.

### Full-illustration scenario

Create a bright, inviting morning panorama of Poppywick Village. Milo rides his red bicycle down a curving cobbled lane, his red satchel full of colourful envelopes and his blue cap slightly tilted by the breeze. Cottages with round doors and flower boxes sit on either side; a bakery, a little post office, a distant windmill, and rolling hills make the village feel abundant and lived-in. Bunny waves from the bakery, Bear waters daisies, and Mouse peeks from a window. Leave a clean patch of pale blue sky in the upper third for the title. The mood is bustling, safe, and welcoming.

### Exact text for the page

**Title:** *Milo the Village Postcat*

**Optional cover line:** A little letter with a very big thank-you.

## Page 2 — The last letter

### Story concept

Milo begins his familiar morning route, delivering letters all over Poppywick. When his satchel is nearly empty, he discovers one tiny envelope that has no name or address. His ordinary day becomes a small mystery.

### Characters in the illustration

- **Milo:** Stopped beside his bicycle, looking gently puzzled as he holds the tiny envelope.
- **Bunny:** Outside the bakery, receiving a postcard from Milo with a delighted expression.
- **Bear:** In his garden, holding a letter and smiling.

### Full-illustration scenario

Show a sunny village lane at mid-morning. Milo’s red bicycle leans against a low stone wall beside him. A few open mailbox flags and happy neighbours in the background show that he has been busy: Bunny clutches a postcard outside the bakery, while Bear reads a letter beneath an apple tree. In the foreground, Milo peers into his almost-empty red satchel and lifts out a single tiny cream envelope. It has a bright red wax seal but a completely blank front. His ears tilt forward with curiosity. Keep a quiet, light-coloured area beside Milo for the narration.

### Exact text for the page

Every morning, Milo delivered letters all around Poppywick.

But at the bottom of his bag was one tiny envelope.

No name. No address.

“Oh dear,” said Milo. “Where does this letter belong?”

### Dialogue treatment

- Put Milo’s final sentence in **one speech bubble** beside his face.
- Keep the first three lines as **narration** in a simple text block.

## Page 3 — Asking the neighbours

### Story concept

Milo asks his friends for help. Their playful guesses make the search feel funny rather than worrying, while showing that the village is a caring community.

### Characters in the illustration

- **Milo:** Holding up the envelope so each friend can see it.
- **Bunny:** Still in her yellow bakery apron; energetic and imaginative.
- **Bear:** In his green gardening hat; thoughtful and calm.
- **Mouse:** Tiny but confident, wearing a violet scarf.

### Full-illustration scenario

Set the scene at the sunny village crossroads, where three lanes meet around a small fountain shaped like a fish. Milo stands in the centre holding the little cream envelope high. Bunny leans forward eagerly, pointing toward the bakery. Bear scratches his chin and points upward at a cloud shaped a little like a moon. Mouse stands on a smooth fountain stone with one paw raised, suggesting an even sillier idea. The red bicycle rests nearby, and a few friendly birds watch from a signpost. The composition should have space around each speaking character for a small, clear speech bubble; keep the action lively but easy to read.

### Exact text for the page

Milo asked his neighbours.

“Is it for the bakery?” asked Bunny.

“Could it be for the moon?” wondered Bear.

“Maybe it is for a mouse!” squeaked Mouse.

Milo smiled. “Let’s look for a clue.”

### Dialogue treatment

- Put Bunny’s, Bear’s, and Mouse’s lines in **separate speech bubbles** by each character.
- Put Milo’s final line in **one speech bubble** beside Milo.
- Keep “Milo asked his neighbours.” as **narration**.

## Page 4 — A little paw-print clue

### Story concept

The friends look carefully together. Milo notices a small paw print beside the wax seal—one that looks just like his own. He begins to understand that the letter may be meant for him.

### Characters in the illustration

- **Milo:** Seated on the cobbles, holding the envelope close and comparing its paw print to his own paw.
- **Bunny, Bear, and Mouse:** Gathered closely around him, sharing the moment with encouraging, expectant faces.

### Full-illustration scenario

Create a quiet, intimate scene beneath a large apple tree at the edge of the village green. The four friends form a soft circle around the envelope. Milo has turned it over and discovered a tiny ginger-coloured paw print pressed next to the red seal. He holds up one front paw beside it in surprise; the marks clearly match. Bunny’s ears lift, Bear gives a knowing smile, and Mouse clasps her paws with excitement. A few apples, daisies, and Milo’s parked red bicycle make the scene feel cozy. Use warm afternoon light and leave a calm patch of grass or sky for the narration.

### Exact text for the page

Then Milo saw a little paw print beside the seal.

He held up his own paw.

It was a perfect match!

“This letter might be for me,” Milo whispered.

### Dialogue treatment

- Put Milo’s final sentence in **one small, gentle speech bubble**.
- Keep the first three lines as **narration**.

## Page 5 — The letter with a big thank-you

### Story concept

Milo opens the letter and discovers a thank-you from every neighbour he helps. The village surprises him with affection, giving the story its warm emotional payoff and showing that kindness comes back around.

### Characters in the illustration

- **Milo:** Centre stage, reading the note with happy, watery eyes; his tail curves into a heart-like shape.
- **Bunny, Bear, and Mouse:** Standing close, proudly part of the surprise.
- **Village neighbours:** A small group of friendly animals—such as a hedgehog, duck, squirrel, and goat—arriving with flowers, cookies, and a little parcel.

### Full-illustration scenario

Set the finale in the village square during golden late-afternoon light. Milo sits on a small wooden bench beside his bicycle, holding the open tiny letter. The note is visible enough to hint at its message, but the printed page text should remain readable separately. Bunny presents a flower garland, Bear holds a tray of honey biscuits, Mouse carries a miniature parcel tied with red string, and other animal neighbours gather around with warm smiles. Loose envelopes swirl playfully in the breeze; the post office and glowing cottage windows sit behind them. The overall image should feel like a hug: celebratory but not loud. Reserve a soft, uncluttered area in the upper sky or foreground for the narration.

### Exact text for the page

Inside, Milo found a note from all his friends:

“Thank you for bringing smiles to Poppywick every day!”

Milo’s heart felt warm and bright.

“The best letters,” he said, “are full of love.”

### Dialogue treatment

- Put the friends’ thank-you message on the **open letter itself** in large, highly legible lettering.
- Put Milo’s final sentence in **one speech bubble**.
- Keep the other lines as **narration**.
