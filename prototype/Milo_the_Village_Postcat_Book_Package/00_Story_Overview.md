## One-line plot

On his usual morning route, Milo the postcat finds a tiny unaddressed letter; with the help of his friends, he discovers it is a village-wide thank-you meant for him.

## Story heart

This is a story about a child-recognisable feeling: doing a kind thing every day and wondering whether anyone notices. Milo is loved because he makes each neighbour feel remembered. The surprise letter lets the village say that kindness matters—even the ordinary, repeated kind of kindness.

The mystery is gentle. Nothing is lost, no one is in danger, and there is no villain. The tension is simply: *Who is waiting for this little letter?*

## Reading experience

- **Audience:** Ages 3–5.
- **Length:** One cover and five story pages only. Each story page has 18–38 words of visible text.
- **Voice:** Clear, musical, and concrete. Read it slowly; leave a small pause after the short mystery lines.
- **Picture role:** The illustrations carry the village detail, the friends’ expressions, and the quiet clues. Text never needs to explain what the picture can show.
- **Dialogue:** Use only one short speech bubble when it adds a child-friendly voice or points attention to an image clue. All other text is narration.

## Complete read-aloud manuscript

### Cover

**Title:** *Milo the Village Postcat*

### Story page 1

Each morning, Milo’s bell went **ting!**

He carried birthday wishes, recipes, and hello-from-far-away letters.

Milo knew just where each one belonged.

And every doorstep felt brighter when he came by.

### Story page 2

At the very bottom of his bag, one tiny envelope waited.

No name.

No house.

Only a red seal, tight and round.

“Someone is waiting for this,” said Milo.

### Story page 3

Bunny checked the bakery.

Bear checked the garden.

Mouse checked the fountain.

But nobody was waiting for a tiny letter.

Then Mouse peeped, “Look!”

### Story page 4

Beside the red seal was the smallest paw print.

Milo held up his own paw.

The two little prints matched.

“This one is for you, Milo,” said Bunny.

### Story page 5

Milo opened the letter.

“Thank you for making Poppywick feel like home,” it said.

Milo’s tail curled into a heart.

“Then tomorrow,” he said, “I’ll bring even more smiles!”

## Visual continuity across all pages

- **Place and time:** One sunny late-spring day in Poppywick Village. The light moves from fresh morning on the cover and Page 1, to warm midday on Pages 2–3, and golden late afternoon on Pages 4–5.
- **Milo’s essentials:** Warm ginger tabby fur; exactly three russet forehead stripes; leaf-green eyes; navy cap with gold paw badge; cream shirt; red diagonal satchel. See the character bible for the full definition.
- **Envelope:** Cream paper, landscape orientation, roughly the size of Milo’s paw. It has a round red wax seal. Its front stays blank on Pages 2–4. On Page 4, a tiny ginger paw print is visible beside the seal. On Page 5, it opens into a cream note with the thank-you message.
- **Illustration density:** Full-page scenes should feel rich but readable: one unmistakable foreground action, two or three supporting midground details, and a quiet background that establishes Poppywick. Do not cram the page with unrelated animal activity.
- **Text legibility:** Reserve a deliberately calm text area in every image. No text over faces, hands/paws, the envelope, or the main emotional action.
