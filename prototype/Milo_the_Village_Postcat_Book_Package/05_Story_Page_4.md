# Page 5 of 6 — Story Page 4: The Letter Is for Milo

## Page purpose

Give the reveal room to land. Milo should realise the letter is for him through a simple visual comparison, while his friends’ warm expressions confirm the happy secret.

## Story moment

At the fountain, Milo compares the little mark on the seal to his own paw. The match tells him the unaddressed letter is meant for him.

## Characters present

- **Milo:** Large foreground focus. He is surprised and moved, not sad: shiny green eyes, a small closed smile beginning, and a tail softly curling near his feet.
- **Bunny:** Near Milo’s shoulder, smiling with gentle anticipation. White rabbit, yellow apron.
- **Bear:** Behind the smaller friends, kneeling down. Calm crescent smile; green hat with orange flower and green apron.
- **Mouse:** On a small smooth fountain stone. Proud posture, paws clasped, violet scarf visible.

## Illustration brief

### Composition

Use a closer crop than Page 4. Milo fills the lower centre, with his left front paw raised beside the envelope in his right paw. The envelope and Milo’s paw are at the same height and form the clear central comparison. Bunny, Bear, and Mouse frame him in a shallow arc behind, leaving the upper-right quarter calm for text.

### Foreground action

The tiny ginger paw print beside the red seal precisely matches the size and shape of Milo’s raised paw. Keep both prints easy to see: show Milo’s pale cream paw tip and ginger fur around it, then the miniature ginger mark on the seal. Milo’s ears are forward and his expression says, “Oh!” Bunny’s face is warm and knowing, but not smug.

### Midground and background

The fountain edge, one apple tree, and the distant curved roof of the post office are enough. The red bicycle rests at the far left edge, partially cropped; it signals that this is still Milo’s delivery day. Avoid props that create visual clutter.

### Light, palette, and mood

The light begins to turn golden, signalling that the day is moving toward its happy ending. Keep the background softly blurred or simplified so the paw comparison is unmistakable. The mood is intimate, affirming, and full of a quiet surprise.

### Complexity limit

**Low-medium.** Four characters, one central comparison, and three understated background elements. No new villagers and no celebration items yet.

## Exact page text

Beside the red seal was the smallest paw print.

Milo held up his own paw.

The two little prints matched.

“This one is for you, Milo,” said Bunny.

## Text treatment and placement

- The first three sentences are narration in the calm upper-right area.
- Bunny’s line goes in a single small speech bubble near her face; it should not cover Milo’s ear or the envelope.
- Do not put words on the envelope itself until the final page.

## Continuity check

- The envelope remains closed and blank except for its red seal and matching ginger paw print.
- Milo’s cap and red satchel remain on him, even in the close crop.
- All friends keep their fixed character-bible colours and proportions.
